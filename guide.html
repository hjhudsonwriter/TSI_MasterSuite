1. Opening the suite

  • Keep the whole suite folder together. Double-click index.html and it opens in Edge. It needs no internet and nothing to install.
  • The home screen shows the eight tools in four groups: DM Tool, World, Players and Set Pieces. Click a card to open its tool.
  • Every tool has the same bar along the top:
    • the crest on the left takes you back to the home screen;
    • Switch tool opens another tool; the ↗ beside a tool opens it in a new Edge window instead, which you can drag to the TV;
    • DM doc opens your DM doc, a panel for your eyes only that floats over the tool: drag it by its title bar, resize it from its bottom-right corner, and close it with ✕. It shows where the campaign stands, read from the Explorer and the Bastion: the party's level and heroes, the day, the Bastion's orders pending (and the day the next completes), the next word from the Ironbow (when, and what it will say), the Region's Clan with its Political Capital and Honour/Respect (and, under them, any open restriction, privilege, favour, obligation or quest hook the Hall's Diplomatic Records name for that Clan, with the day it ends: these are for you to run at the table, the suite doesn't enforce them; changes to Honour/Respect or Political Capital are applied by the Bastion itself and aren't listed), the Region's god with its Favour, the party's event gold, and the Explorer's Active Effects and Threads (with what resolving each gives). It keeps itself up to date, even when the Explorer and the Bastion are open in other windows. Pop out (in its title bar) opens it in a window of its own that you can drag onto another screen, such as the TV. Change a score… sets a Clan's Political Capital (−100 to 100) or Honour/Respect (−5 to 5), or a god's Favour (0 to 100%), to the number you type. The DM doc never writes the Bastion's save itself: it keeps a short list of changes, and the Bastion applies each one once, the next time it is open (straight away if it already is), and writes what the score was and became in its Day Log. Under the button each change says Waiting until the Bastion has applied it;
    • Export and Import (only in tools that save) copy that tool's data to or from a file;
    • Saved ✓ shows your work is saved.
  • Leaving the Heartwood Ritual, the Pelagosi puzzles or an Arenas round part-way asks first, because they can't be picked up again later.

2. Your two screens

  • The TV extends the laptop's screen. Drag a window across, or press Windows + Shift + ← or → to move the window you're in to the other screen.
  • F11 makes Edge fill the screen. Press it again to go back.
  • Two tools open a window for the players: the Combat Tracker's Battlemap and the Notice Board's Pop-out Board. Drag that window to the TV. It closes when you leave its tool.
  • In the Explorer, press Fullscreen, then H (or Hide UI) to show only the map. H again brings the panels back.
  • The tools are sized for the laptop at 150% and the TV at 100%. If one looks cut off, check Scale in Windows' Display settings for that screen.

3. Saving and backups

Please read this one. Your saves live inside Edge on this laptop, not in the suite's folder.

  • The Combat Tracker, Notice Board, Explorer, Bastion, Arenas and Clan Crest Creator save as you go (the Crest remembers your last design). The Heartwood Ritual and the Pelagosi puzzles save nothing, as before.
  • Because saves live in Edge:
    • another browser, another computer or an InPrivate window won't see them;
    • clearing Edge's browsing data (Cookies and other site data) deletes them, and so does Edge's setting to clear that data whenever Edge closes, if it's switched on;
    • every copy of the suite on this laptop shares the same saves. Moving or renaming the folder is fine. But a new version you download to try out opens your real saves too, so back up first.
  • Back up everything (on the home screen) puts one file, named tsi-backup-everything- and the date, in your Downloads folder. Do it after each session, and keep the files somewhere safe, such as OneDrive or a USB stick.
  • Restore (on the home screen) loads a backup file. It shows what's in the file and asks before replacing anything.
  • For one tool, use Export and Import in its top bar. Import asks first, and offers to save a copy of what's there now. The Explorer and the Bastion share one campaign, so their Export and Import carry both together, in one file named tsi-campaign- and the date.
  • If a save ever can't be read, the tool sets it aside (it isn't deleted) and starts fresh. Restore a backup, or ask a session to recover the copy that was set aside.

4. The tools at the table

Combat Tracker & VTT Battlemap DM Tool Open ›

Before the session

  • Library: fill in Add Combatant (name, type, max HP, and if you like the initiative bonus, a picture and a stat-block link), then Add to Library. Click a saved combatant to select it; Shift + click edits it.
  • Select the ones for this fight and press Add Selected. Copies are named Goblin, Goblin a, and so on. If you add more during a fight, it carries on: the round and whose turn it is don't change, and the new arrivals wait at the end of the list (skipped) until you type an initiative in their row and press Enter, or press Roll for new arrivals, which rolls d20 plus their bonus for them alone and slots them into the order. Name the encounter in the middle panel; Encounters › Save Current Encounter keeps it for another night.
  • Import/Export: Export Campaign JSON and Import Campaign JSON move your whole library to or from a file. Import PDF (basic) tries to read a name and HP from a PDF.

In the fight

  • Auto-roll Initiative, then Begin Encounter.
  • On each turn, pick the Target, type the Damage / Healing (12 for damage, −5 to heal), add a condition and its turns if needed, then press Complete Turn.
  • Pause and End stop the fight. Begin after a Pause starts again at round 1, as before.
  • Hide Monsters hides every monster from the Battlemap (and from you); Reveal Monsters shows them again.
  • Battlemap opens the map window. Upload battlemap (up to 4 MB), drag the tokens, and use the grid, Snap, Fog and the ruler (📏). Drag the window to the TV and press Fullscreen.

A fight from the Explorer

  • When the Explorer sends a fight, the tracker offers it: Load the fight puts its monsters in the encounter (and the library), and its battle map, grid and starting places on the Battlemap. Your PCs stay; heroes caught by an ambush are marked Surprised for their first turn. Then Auto-roll Initiative and Begin Encounter.
  • After Not now, Load the Explorer's fight at the top offers it again.
  • When every monster is down, the tracker tells the Explorer, where you press Won.

Notice Board Quest Generator DM Tool Open ›

  • Set the filters (level, how many, type, province, faction and honour), then press Generate. The notices appear on the board.
  • Each notice has Accept and Decline. Accepted quests move to Accepted Quests, grouped by province, with their outline. ✕ takes one off the list.
  • ★ marks the main quest. With Wi-Fi on, it also tells Matt Owen's Knightly Treasures shop which quest is active; offline it skips quietly and catches up next time. Open shop ↗ opens the shop (it needs the internet).
  • Pop-out Board opens the players' window. Drag it to the TV; it shows the notices only.
  • Clear empties the board.

The Ironbow Bastion Manager DM Tool Open ›

  • The Bastion map fills the window. Its top bar shows the day, the treasury (type the gold and press Enter, or click away), how many Facilities are built and how many Orders are pending: hover over either for the list, or click it for its panel. The badge in the map's top-right corner opens Party Identity. Along the bottom, either side of the facility tiles, are buttons: Warehouse, Management, Day Log and Events on the left; Clan Influence, Favour and the War Council on the right. Each opens its panel over the map; Close (or Esc) closes it. The small arrow above the tiles folds them away to show more of the map. While the tiles are showing, the painting takes on a soft haze and each built facility has a small label with its name and level; folding the tiles away removes both. Party Level, Compendium, Reset and the save buttons are in the slim bar above the map. Reset, and every Clear or Remove that would lose something, ask first; Reset offers to download a copy before it clears. Each upgrade row says what the next level unlocks.
  • Along the bottom are the facility tiles, in two rows: the five you start with and six construction slots above, and six Development Plots below (with a sprout). Hover over a tile to see its level and the orders pending there; click it to open its panel. A slot with a + is free: click it, choose a facility (hover over one to see what it does and how many days it takes to build), and confirm with Construct. Building takes 21 to 35 days, and the tile shows an hourglass and the days left; click it if you want to stop building. A padlocked slot opens at a higher party level (its tooltip says which).
  • Development Plots are only for the production facilities: the Herbal Gardens, the Timber Yard & Sawmill, the Fishery & Smokehouse, the Stone Quarry & Masonworks, the Weavers' Hall and the Courier Exchange. There are none until party level 9, then 2, 4 at level 13 and 6 at level 17. Click a free plot to build one (21 days); the other facilities still go in the construction slots. An Herbal Gardens built in a slot before plots existed moves to a free plot by itself, keeping its level, orders and research, and the Day Log says so.
  • The Stone Quarry & Masonworks and the Weavers' Hall (Level 5, one production order at a time, no gold) work like the Timber Yard: the quarry extracts Raw Stone, then dresses it into Dressed Stone, Structural Masonry or Decorative Stonework, with two bulk orders at Level 5; the Hall makes Ordinary Textiles, then Sailcloth and Specialist Garments (two lots at a time at Level 4), with two bulk orders at Level 5. Processing orders take their ingredients out of the Warehouse when you issue them. Everything they make is trade cargo, counted in lots, to ship from the Dock; none of it goes to the party.
  • Goods: the production facilities make goods, such as Raw Timber, Fresh Fish, Restorative Herbs or Expedition Provision Packs. Each order says what it Uses (and how much the Warehouse has) and what it Makes. What it uses leaves the Warehouse when you issue it, and the Orders panel shows what it's holding; Cancel (or Demolish) puts it back. The Timber Yard and the Fishery run one production order at a time; if an order can't be given, its button says "Can't order yet" and the reason is underneath. In the Warehouse, goods have their own rows, showing what one lot holds and their value (the base value per lot; trade goods become gold when a Dock ship sells them: see Port Charters and cargo below). You can change their count and notes; a good that's used up stays at 0 until you Remove it.
  • To knock a facility down, open its panel and press Demolish… at the foot (the Hall's is beside Clear Diplomacy Records). It asks first. Its slot (or plot) is freed and any orders pending there are cancelled, with no gold back (goods they were holding go back to the Warehouse); what it already made stays. The five starting facilities can't be demolished.
  • Every facility has a level, from 1 to 5, shown at the top of its panel. To raise it, press the Upgrade box's button (it names the next level), the first of its orders (the Hall's Upgrade is beside its level badge). It costs no gold and takes 7, 14, 21 or 28 days to reach Level 2, 3, 4 or 5. It waits under "Orders pending here" like any order (Cancel stops it). One upgrade at a time, and not while the facility is Under Repair. A level opens new orders at the Herbal Gardens, the Timber Yard and the Fishery (an order that needs a higher level says so on its button), and the Hall's bonus and actions; what other facilities' levels unlock comes later.
  • Research (the Herbal Gardens, for now): from Level 2, a facility earns a research token at each level. The Research box in its panel shows the tokens free; Open Research shows its four branches. Each stage offers two choices: take one with its Research button (it asks first, and costs the gold shown and a token; Stage I takes 7 days, II 14, III 21). A stage opens once the one before it on its branch is done, and one project runs at a time. Undo on a researched choice gives its token back (not the gold). Choices marked "Coming later" wait for parts of the Bastion still to be built; hover over any choice to read it in full.
  • The facilities changed on 13 October 2026. The Arcane Study and Laboratory are now one Arcane Laboratory, the Garden and Greenhouse one Herbal Gardens, and the Library is the Library & Scriptorium; the Laboratory's and Greenhouse's orders (Alchemy and Greenhouse Harvest) still need party level 9. The Smithy has joined the Workshop, and the Gaming Hall is gone. The new facilities are listed in Construction as In development: you can see them, and each can be built once its orders are ready. A Bastion saved before the change is converted the first time it opens: a notice and the Day Log say what changed, and a copy of the Bastion as it was is kept aside (it's in Back up everything).
  • A facility's panel has its picture, its level, the orders pending there and its orders, each with how many days it takes: pick a choice and press Issue Order. The gold is taken straight away. The Workshop's panel also holds the Artisan Tools its Craft list uses. The Hall of Emissaries' tile opens the Hall (below).
  • The Bastion follows the Explorer's day: there is no button to move time on. These are game days, not real ones: real time never moves the Bastion on, and a day passes only when the party makes camp. Each time the party makes camp in the Explorer, the Bastion passes that day, even in another window: orders due that day complete, building work finishes, contracts send their shipments, and every 28 days (Days 29, 57 and so on) a Bastion event is rolled. The top bar shows the Bastion's day. If you open the Bastion after several days of travel, it passes each day in turn. If you use Reset Travel in the Explorer, the Bastion's days move back with it.
  • The Ironbow sends word… When a day brings news (building or an order finished, an agreement ending, or something that needs you), a pop-up says so in the Bastion, and in the Explorer when the party makes camp (see the Explorer below). The Day Log keeps every day's news.
  • When it needs a d20, type the roll made at the table (or press Roll 1d20), then Continue. If you cancel a roll, that order says "Due now: waiting for your roll" in the Orders panel and its facility's panel: press its Resolve when you're ready. If Edge closes part-way through a day, reopen the Bastion: it finishes the day by itself, or press Finish Day.
  • Once the Hall of Emissaries is built, its tile opens your diplomacy. A Trade Agreement lasts the weeks you choose (1, 3 or 6) and sends a shipment every 7 days; the Hall shows the days remaining and the shipments left. Routes shows the Sea Trade Routes map, Resolve settles the trade routes due to sail (each sails every 7 days), and Council Ledger settles disputes. These agreements and sea routes are unchanged by the cargo trade below: they still pay their fixed income, and never carry goods. The Secure Trade Agreement is retired (20 October 2026) and no longer appears in the Hall: Register Port Charter now has its place, first in the list and open from Level 1. An agreement already running finishes and pays as before, and shows in the Hall's records until it ends. The Trade Consortium is unchanged.
  • The Hall also keeps your campaign's diplomatic history. Clan Relations lists all seven Clans with the Bastion's own Honour & Respect and Political Capital, and any restrictions, privileges or favours still running. Diplomatic Records is a ledger you write: press Record an event…, give it a title, tick the Clans involved, set the in-game day, and write the situation, what the players decided and the outcome. Add consequences if you want: a change to Honour & Respect or Political Capital (these really change the Clan's score), or a restriction or embargo, a privilege, a favour owed, an unresolved obligation or a quest hook (you type what it is and how many days it lasts; the Bastion shows it and counts the days, but does not enforce it). Save draft keeps it for later and changes nothing. Confirm outcome says exactly what it will do, then does it once. A confirmed record can't be edited, but you can add a note, or end a lasting consequence early. Nothing is preset or random, and the old Clear Diplomacy Records button leaves this ledger alone.
  • Port Charters and cargo (from 14 October 2026). A Port Charter lets the Dock's ships sell real goods at a port: ten ports: Timberport (Clan Blackstone), Goldport (Clan Molten), Port Brawdlyn (Clan Rowthorn), Steelport (Clan Slade), Moltenport (Clan Molten), Leatherport and Bleakharbour (Clan Karr), Alderbridge (Clan Blackstone), Redport (Clan Farmer) and The River Rook (Clan Bacca's river port, since Midland Province has no harbour). Sailing: Timberport and Goldport 7 days out and 7 back; Port Brawdlyn 2, Steelport 3, Moltenport 4, The River Rook 4, Leatherport 5, Bleakharbour 6, Alderbridge 7 and Redport 9, each way. The longer the voyage, the higher the risk: 5, 10, 20, and Redport 30 in 100.
    • Getting one: when the party wins one at the table, grant it in the Explorer (see below). Within a few seconds the Bastion holds it: a notice says so, and Changes from the Explorer in Clan Influence lists it. Then register it at the Hall of Emissaries: Register Port Charter (Hall Level 2) takes 3 days, costs nothing and needs no roll; the Hall registers one Charter at a time. The Hall's Port Charters box shows each one: earned, being registered, or registered.
    • Shipping: open the Dock. Its Cargo and Port Charters section shows what the Dock can do at its level: nothing at Level 1; one ship carrying 2, 3 or 4 lots at Levels 2, 3 and 4; and two ships of 5 lots at Level 5. (The Charter Berth order above it is unchanged.) Press Ship cargo…, choose the port and how many lots of each good to send, and check the quote: each lot's base value × how much the buying Clan wants that good, less any shipping fee and tariff (none for now). Each Clan has three goods it highly seeks (×1.25) and three it wants moderately (×1.10); everything else is Normal (×1.00). For example, Clan Molten at Goldport highly seeks Medicinal Herbs, Specialist Garments and Rare Botanical Extracts, and Clan Blackstone at Timberport highly seeks Sawn Lumber, Construction Timber and Premium Hardwood. Press Send the ship (it's greyed out, with the reason beside the lot count, if the ship can't take that cargo): the goods leave the Warehouse straight away. Fresh Fish ships only on a voyage of 2 days or less (it doesn't go off in the Warehouse); today's ports are 7 days away, so it stays at home. The Dock says why: beside Ship cargo… when Fresh Fish is the only trade good in stock, or on its row in the Ship cargo pop-up.
    • At sea: the ship reaches the port after that port's sailing days (7 for Timberport and Goldport) and comes home as many days after that, and can't sail again until it's back. When it arrives, the Bastion rolls the voyage's risk by itself (the port's own chance, listed above): no roll for you. Usually the cargo sells, and the gold goes into the treasury once, worked out with that day's prices. If there's trouble at sea, nothing sells, and the cargo comes home with the ship and goes back into the Warehouse. The Day Log, The Ironbow sends word… and the Dock's Recent sales say what happened; the Explorer's word says beforehand when a ship arrives or is home, never what it will sell for. Each sale is also noted (port, good, lots and day) for market prices later; for now it doesn't change any price.
    • Recall ship…: under Ships at sea, a ship still on its way to the port can be called back (it asks first). It turns for home at once and takes as many days to get back as it has sailed so far (at least 1). Nothing is sold and there's no risk roll; its cargo goes back into the Warehouse when it docks, and the ship can sail again then. Once a ship has reached the port, it can't be recalled. Recalling a standing shipment's ship doesn't stop the standing shipment.
    • Standing shipments (Dock Level 4): Set up a standing shipment… sends the same cargo every 7 days, starting the next day, whenever a ship is home and the goods and the room are there; otherwise it waits (the Day Log says why, once) and sails the first day it can. With one ship and a 14-day round trip, that's every 14 days in practice. Two standing shipments sharing a ship take turns. Stop ends it.
    • Market saturation: a port gets glutted. Within 28 days, at one port, a good's first 4 lots sell at the Clan's demand, the next 4 one step lower, the next 4 two lower, and from the 13th it is Oversupplied (×0.75). The Dock's preview counts sales already made, not ships still at sea.
    • Every number (prices, demand, fees, tariffs, days at sea, risks, the Dock's room) is in tools/bastion/data/commerce-data.js, ready to change.
    • Negotiated Charter terms (from 20 October 2026): in the Hall's Port Charters box each Charter shows its terms (price multiplier, shipping fee per lot, tariff; standard is ×1.00, 0 gp, 0%) and an estimate of what 4 lots would fetch. Amend terms… sets them from what happened in the story: price ×0.50 to ×2.00, fee 0 to 50 gp a lot, tariff 0 to 50%, and a reason is required. Worse-than-standard terms are allowed if you choose them. Reset to standard terms… also asks why. Every change is kept in the Charter's history and the Day Log; terms never expire by themselves, and a ship already at sea keeps the terms it sailed with. Nothing here changes Honour, Respect or Political Capital.
    • Protection for voyages: the Barracks' Guard Commercial Convoy (Level 2, 3 days, free) prepares one Convoy for the next ship (−5 points of risk); the Watchtower's Protect Maritime Approaches (Level 2, 2 days, free) covers every ship that sails in the next 7 days (the day it completes is Day 1; −5 points). With the Blessing of Pelagos (−10) they add up, never below 0. They apply by themselves, standing shipments included, and the Dock lists what is ready or active.
  • The Courier Exchange (from 14 October 2026) sends Expedition Provision Packs and Overland charts from the Warehouse to a town, where they wait until the party collects them in the Explorer (see the Explorer below). It is built on a Development Plot (from party level 9, in 21 days) and has nothing to do with the Dock: no ships, no Port Charters.
    • Where it reaches: at Level 1, Slade’s Muster (2 days, 5 gp). Level 2 adds Moorcastle (4 days, 10 gp), Bretan (5 days, 10 gp), Middlemount (6 days, 20 gp), Timberport and Goldport (7 days, 20 gp each), Swyth (10 days, 30 gp) and Bleakharbour (12 days, 30 gp). At Level 1 one parcel can be travelling at a time, with up to 5 items; at Level 2, two at a time with up to 10 items each. A pack or a chart is one item.
    • More by parcel (from 20 October 2026): the Courier Exchange also sends Restorative Herbs, Antivenom Roots, Toxic Blossoms and Alchemical Flowers (one portion counts as one item), and any item you typed into the Warehouse yourself. Typed items appear under Typed into the Warehouse in the Send dialog, each as its own line; the Travel bags show the exact name and count, and you handle any effect at the table. Trade lots, ships and creatures never go by courier. Sizes, fees and days are unchanged.
    • Open the Courier Exchange and press Send a parcel…. Choose the town and how many of each to send; the line underneath says how many items that is, the fee, and the day it reaches the town. Send the parcel is greyed out, with the reason, if it can't go (too many items, too few in the Warehouse, too little gold, no room, or the Courier Exchange Under Repair). When it goes, the items leave the Warehouse and the fee leaves the treasury, once. The fee is for the parcel, however full it is.
    • The panel lists parcels Travelling, Returning to Ironbow and Waiting for collection, and the ones recently closed. On its arrival day a parcel waits at its town (the Day Log and "The Ironbow sends word…" say so) for as long as it takes; a waiting parcel no longer takes up a travelling place. Nothing goes wrong on the road.
    • Recall parcel… calls back a parcel that's still travelling (it asks first). It turns round and takes as many days to come home as it has travelled so far, at least 1; then its items go back into the Warehouse. The fee isn't refunded. A parcel that has reached its town can't be recalled.
    • + Add supplies in the Warehouse puts packs or charts in by hand, for now. The Ironbow also makes them itself: the Fishery & Smokehouse makes packs, and the Library & Scriptorium charts (below). It makes no gold, and the Day Log records it.
    • A parcel already sent carries on if the Courier Exchange is Under Repair or demolished; it still arrives and can be collected. While it's Under Repair you can still recall a parcel, but not once it's demolished. Levels 3 to 5 (return trips from the party, priority and express parcels) come later. Every number is in tools/bastion/data/courier-data.js, ready to change.
  • The Library & Scriptorium's Prepare Overland Travel Charts (from 15 October 2026) needs the Library at Level 2: it makes 1 Overland chart in 4 days, with no gold, straight into the Warehouse, one at a time. Its scripture Research order is unchanged and can run at the same time. The Courier Exchange can send the charts to the party.
  • Creatures (from 15 October 2026). Three buildings house real, named animals, each in a construction slot (from party level 5, 21 days to build); building one never creates an animal:
    • The Wyvern Roost: its order House Elara's Wyvern (3 days, no gold, once ever) records Elara's own wyvern at the Ironbow. It's shown as "Elara's wyvern" until it has a name. The Roost houses one more wyvern than its level (Elara's is one of them: 2 at Level 1, up to 6 at Level 5), and its order House a Wyvern gives a home to a wyvern the DM granted in the Explorer, exactly as the other two buildings do. Building or upgrading the Roost never makes a wyvern.
    • The Griffin Tower and Dire Wolf Den: a griffin or a dire wolf comes only when the DM grants one in the Explorer (Grant a creature…, see the Explorer below). The Bastion then holds it, waiting for a home: a notice says so, and Clan Influence's Changes from the Explorer lists it. The building's order (House a Griffin or House a Dire Wolf: choose which, 3 days, no gold) houses it. Each level of the building houses one: a Level 1 Griffin Tower houses one griffin, and its Upgrade adds room for another.
    • Each building's panel has a Creatures section: the room, those Living here, those Waiting for a home, and what's coming later. Hover over a creature to see the stats it fights with. Management lists every creature too. A griffin or dire wolf still waiting for a home has Release…, for one granted by mistake: it asks first, and the creature leaves for good.
    • Every creature in its building's panel has a Rename… button, whether it is housed or still waiting: type a name (up to 40 letters), or leave it empty for the usual one. This is the way to name Elara's wyvern once you know its name; the new name shows in the panels, Management, the War Table and the Day Log.
    • Creatures defend the Ironbow when it's attacked (Defend Bastion battles) and are never sent on a War Action. Elara's wyvern fights with Battle Value 11 and Flight, a griffin 8 and Flight, a dire wolf 7. A creature is never killed or captured: at worst it's badly wounded for 21 days. Creatures don't unlock the War Council by themselves, and the Menagerie is unchanged.
    • Demolishing a creature's building leaves its creatures at the Ironbow, still guarding it; rebuilding gives them a home again. Care, training, bonding, riding and breeding come later.
  • The Warehouse and Artisan Tools save as you type. Sort (beside Add Item) can group the list: supplies together, then ingredients, trade goods and general items (the rows you typed); it only changes how they are shown, and it's remembered. On a row you typed by hand, Hand to the party asks how many and who gets them, then takes only that many out of the Warehouse (once) and writes it in the Day Log. The suite doesn't keep a character's inventory, so write the item on that character's D&D sheet yourself; goods the Ironbow makes, and anything delivered from far away, go by the Courier Exchange instead. Compendium lists every item the Bastion can make. Clan Influence shows each Clan's Political Capital, its Honour/Respect (yours to set, −5 to +5) and the support they add up to, and your Favour Tokens. Eight Explorer event moments also change a Clan's Honour/Respect by 1 by themselves (see the Explorer below), once each: when the Bastion reaches the day it happened, or straight away if it's already there. A notice says so, the Day Log keeps it, and Changes from the Explorer in Clan Influence lists the latest ten. If you're typing in a Clan's box as one arrives, both count. Events has Roll Bastion Event, the last event, and when the next automatic one comes.
  • The badge in the map's top-right corner shows the Bastion's crest, or "Add a crest" until it has one; click it for Party Identity. The crest belongs to the Bastion, so you can set it whether the party is Unsworn or a Clan. Create in the Clan Crest Creator ↗ opens the Crest Creator in a new window: design the crest there and press its Use for the Bastion button, and the Bastion asks "Use this crest for the Bastion?" (straight away if it's open, or the next time you open it). Upload a picture… uses a picture file instead, and Remove takes the crest away. The crest also marks your tokens on the War Table.
  • Form Clan needs party level 9, a total support of 360 and three Clans at 55 or more; the requirements line under it says which you have. Its pop-up shows the Bastion's crest, which the Clan keeps: Create a new one ↗ or Upload… chooses another, and Keep this crest goes back to it. Once you're a Clan, Party Identity shows its name, chief and motto, and Clan Honour. (The Mercenary Brigade is no longer part of the Bastion.)
  • Once the War Room is built, its Recruit order takes on a Lieutenant or a unit of soldiers. Open the list and hover over each unit (or move to it with the arrow keys) to see its stat block. New troops join the Military panel. Each regiment is listed on its own, numbered by kind ("Line Infantry II"); Rename… gives it a name of your own. A regiment that came home under strength is Recouping: it refills by itself over 3, 7, 10 or 14 days (by how many it lost), or at once when you recruit its kind again. Hover over anything in Military or Menagerie Beasts (or move to it with the Tab key) to see the stats it fights with, and who is away recovering.
  • The Shrines (from 15 October 2026). Each Shrine of Telluria, Aurush and Pelagos keeps its Craft and Pray, and gains an order at each level from 2 to 5. Orders from Level 3 spend the god's Favour when they start: 5% at Level 3, 10% at Level 4 and 25% for the Level 5 Great Rite; an order without enough says so. Their items (an Earthward Talisman, Rootguard Salve, a Blessed Lantern, Purification Incense, Cleansing Portions, a Voyage Blessing, an Aquatic Protection Charm) go into the Warehouse, and the Courier Exchange can send them to the party. Bless Commercial Shipment keeps a Blessing of Pelagos for the Dock: the next ship sent with cargo takes it, 10 points off its chance of trouble at sea. A Great Rite waits at the Ironbow; the DM gives it to the party on an expedition, in the Explorer. Bless Bastion Foundations stays unavailable for now: no Bastion event damages a facility by natural causes, and none is being added. Each Shrine's panel shows its Favour, and what waits there.
  • War Room training (from 15 October 2026). At Level 2, Drill a Regiment trains one regiment at full strength: Trained (7 days, 25 gp × its Battle Value) gives +1 Resolve and +0.5 Battle Value; Veteran (14 more days, 50 gp × Battle Value) adds +1 Cohesion and +0.5 Battle Value more. The gold is paid when the drill starts. One regiment drills at a time at Level 2, two at Levels 3 and 4, three at Level 5; a drill waits while its regiment is Recouping or away at a battle. A regiment drilling stays home from War Actions but defends the Ironbow if it's attacked. At Level 3, Coordinated Muster raises two formations in one 7-day order; at Level 5, Grand Muster raises three. The War Room's Regiments list shows each one's grade, Recouping and drill.
  • The War Council button stays locked (with a padlock) until the Bastion has something that can fight: a defender from the Barracks, a beast from the Menagerie or a unit from the War Room. To go to war, use War Action in the Banner & War Council panel. Choose the Target Clan, the Objective (Raid, Skirmish or Seize Outpost) and the Enemy force, then type how many of each you'll send: defenders, Lieutenants, and each kind of regiment and beast you have. Hover over a name, or move to its box with the Tab key, to see its stat block. Only a Clan can send Lieutenants and regiments. Intelligence shows the scouts' estimate of the enemy, and Your army shows your strength in Battle Value. Then press Queue War Action.
  • Queueing a War Action declares war on that Clan, or renews the war if you're already at war with it. It costs you Honour & Respect and Political Capital with that Clan at once, by the size of the army you send: −3 and −30 under 20 Battle Value, −4 and −40 from 20 to 39, and −5 and −50 at 40 or more. The line beside the button shows the cost as you type. Queue War Action asks first: press Declare war (or Renew the war), or Cancel to change nothing. If you cancel the War Action in the pending orders on the same day you queued it, what it cost is given back, so you can correct it and queue it again without paying twice.
  • While you're at war, an At War tag follows that Clan's name wherever it shows in the Bastion (in a list, it reads "Bacca (At War)"), and the Wars box in the War Council lists the war. A war ends by itself after 42 days without a battle between you. Make peace ends it sooner, though not while a War Action or a battle with that Clan is still waiting. Making peace doesn't give back what declaring war cost.
  • While you're at war, every 7 days of the war (counted from the day it was declared) the Bastion rolls a d6 for that Clan. On a 1, that Clan attacks (one attack a day at most, and none while an earlier attack still waits): Sound the horns! Everyone free at the Bastion stands to defend it. Press Defend the Ironbow to roll for the weather, morale and luck, then fight on the War Table, on the Ironbow coast map with its terrain already marked. Or press Later and defend it from the War Council when you're ready. An attack can't be called off. If nobody is free to defend the Bastion, press See the War Report: it falls without a battle. If the Watchtower's Patrol finished in the last 7 days, the pop-up reminds you that your Bastion Defenders have Advantage on all their rolls in that battle.
  • Losing a defence, or withdrawing from it, costs 5% to 50% of the treasury (1d10 × 5%), and 1d4 of your facilities are Under Repair for 14 days (the day it's lost included). Their tiles (with a hammer), their panels, the map's top bar and the Orders panel say so, with the day the repairs are done. They take no orders, and orders already running there wait until they're working again. The Day Log says when the repairs are done.
  • When the war comes due, press Begin Military Action. Or press Later, and begin it from the panel when you're ready. Roll for the weather, your forces' morale and their luck, then the War Table opens. There you can Upload battle map, and use Terrain to mark woods, water and cliffs. In snow, rain or a heatwave, the weather's film plays over the battlefield, for show only: Weather: On turns it off, and Weather: Off turns it back on. Then press Begin Deployment, move your forces on your half of the field, and press Start Battle. The DM's button moves the enemy's units, and in a Raid, Defend Bastion or Seize Outpost battle the supplies, your depot or the outpost too. When the battle starts, a briefing says how to win and lose; Rules & objective shows it again at any time.
  • Fight the battle one unit at a time. Click one of your units, then click an enemy to attack it (or pick another order), and press Confirm. On the enemy's turn, press Enemy acts. Withdraw leaves the field and counts as a loss. When the battle ends, the War Report shows what it won and what it cost: soldiers lost, and Lieutenants or beasts away recovering. The Bastion is updated once.
  • Call off ends a Military Action with nothing won or lost, as long as no unit has acted yet. The next attempt at that war faces the same enemy, and keeps any weather, morale and luck already rolled. Wounded Lieutenants and beasts come back after 7 to 21 days: the Bastion logs their return on the day, and the Ironbow sends word.

5. If something goes wrong

  • "Already open" (for example "The Combat Tracker & VTT Battlemap is also open in another tab or window"): the same tool, or the home screen, is open in two tabs. Close one of them, or one can overwrite the other's saves. Two different tools in two tabs, such as the Bastion and the Crest, are fine.
  • "… is open in another window": the Explorer and the Bastion each run in one window at a time, so two copies can never save over each other or count a change from the other twice. A second window waits, perhaps behind the TV's window. Close the tool in the other window and it opens in this one by itself; or press Back to the home screen. A campaign Import and the home screen's Restore also wait until neither is open in another window.
  • A red "Something went wrong" bar: press Reload. Your saved work stays. Details shows the technical message, which helps a session fix it.
  • "Saved (small storage)" in the top bar: Edge is only letting the suite use a small save space. Back up often.
  • An Import is refused: the file is from another tool, or damaged. Nothing was changed.
  • No sound: check the laptop's volume, and in the Ritual press Enable Sound.
  • Web links, pictures or stat blocks from the internet, and the Knightly Treasures shop, need Wi-Fi. Everything else works without it.

6. Getting changes made

  • The tools were rebuilt as they were. Behaviours that were kept on purpose, including a few odd ones, are listed in docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md, each with a line saying what happened to it.
  • To change a rule, fix something or add a feature, start a session and describe it. The notes in docs/ tell the session how the suite is built.
  • Quests, events, facilities, arenas and maps are kept in each tool's data folder, not buried in the code, so they can be changed without touching the rest.
  • Two check pages you can double-click: tests/rules.html runs the rules tests, and tests/pin-check.html shows every Explorer map with all its town pins.
